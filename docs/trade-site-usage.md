# Trade site usage

The trade site API is not in the [Grinding Gear Games developer documentation](https://www.pathofexile.com/developer/docs), and their Terms of Use (section 7i) forbid reverse-engineering undocumented endpoints; the sanction is the closing of the accounts of the developer and of the users. The whole community uses it (Awakened PoE Trade, Exiled Exchange 2) and Grinding Gear Games tolerates it, but it is not allowed. The owner accepted this risk on 2026-10-23, with the guardrails below.

The trade site announces its limits per IP address in the `X-Rate-Limit-*` headers of each response (`hits:period:penalty`, in seconds). Measured on 2026-10-23:

| Policy | Limits |
|---|---|
| Search (`trade-search-request-limit`) | 5 per 10 s (60 s penalty), 15 per 60 s (300 s), 30 per 300 s (1,800 s), 600 per 6 h (3,600 s) |
| Fetch (`trade-fetch-request-limit`), 10 offers per request | 12 per 4 s (10 s penalty), 16 per 12 s (300 s), 50 per 300 s (300 s), 1,000 per 6 h (1,800 s) |
| Currency exchange (`trade-exchange-request-limit`), measured on 2026-10-26 | 5 per 15 s (60 s penalty), 10 per 90 s (300 s), 30 per 300 s (1,800 s) |

The data endpoints (`/api/trade2/data/*`, the item names and the stat templates) announce no limit: one `GET /api/trade2/data/static` on 2026-11-20 answered without `X-Rate-Limit-*` nor `Retry-After`, from the cache of Cloudflare (`cf-cache-status: HIT`, `cache-control: public, max-age=14400`). The application still spaces them a second apart (`PACE` in `src/trade/site.rs`) and reads the `Retry-After` of a refusal of theirs.

Guardrails:

- A search only runs when the player clicks the search or refresh block: copying an item never searches by itself (sorting the loot copies many items in a row). One click makes one search and one fetch; the double chevron makes one more fetch. Nothing runs in the background.
- After each search, the waiting block lasts at least 10 seconds, and longer until every window of the limits allows a new search. The limits are read from the headers of every response (the IP address may share them with a browser or another tool), never from constants; after a refusal (HTTP 429, or 403), the block waits for the `Retry-After` delay (a minute when the refusal gives none), or for the restriction the limits of the refusal tell when it is longer (the third number of a window of `X-Rate-Limit-*-State`), and the application never retries by itself. That wait holds for every request to the trade site: the searches of items, those of the currency exchange, the next offers, and the daily download of the item names, which a refusal of its own (403 or 429) makes wait the same way, an hour at least (security audit of 2026-11-10). The end of that wait is kept in the cache directory (`trade-refusal.json`) and read at the next launch, so that closing the application and launching it again sends no request during the penalty (finding 9 of the same audit, step 22k). The file is read again before each request, and the longest wait is kept, in memory and in the file: two Exile Market that run at once on one cache directory (the lock of the single instance couldn't be taken, or its file was deleted) share the wait, and a shorter one never replaces a longer one (security audit of 2026-11-21, a low finding, step 22p1). No lock is held between the reading and the writing: two of them refused at the same instant may keep the shorter of their two waits in the file, each still waiting for its own. That file keeps the delay of the refusal alone, which every request waits for; the hour at least of a refused download of the names is kept in a file of its own (`item-names-wait.json`), read before each download and written the same way, so that it holds at the next launch and for another Exile Market without making the searches wait an hour (security audit of 2026-11-21, a low finding, step 22p2). A time in the past or more than a day ahead, the longest wait read from a refusal, is ignored. The wait isn't kept when its file can't be written or read (no cache directory, a file of another version), and it follows the clock of the system: a clock moved forward since the refusal ends it early, one set back makes it longer by as much, and ignored once more than a day is left. The headers of a refusal were never read on the real trade site (the limits above come from answers of status 200, and a refusal isn't provoked on purpose): the log writes the status, the policy, the `Retry-After` and the limits and state of each rule of each one, to check them at the first that a player meets. While no name is known, a download that the trade site answers with an error, or with names the application can't read, or stops answering after its first requests, is tried again after 1, 2, 4… minutes, an hour at most, instead of every minute: its six requests would fail the same way each time (quality review of 2026-11-10). No request follows a redirection.
- What the application knows of the limits is kept in the cache directory (`trade-limits.json`) when a search leaves and after each answer, and read at the next launch (step 22p4): the windows told, the times of its requests, the end of a restriction and the time of the last search. Closing the application and launching it again shortens neither the 10 seconds nor a window: before, a new launch knew no limit until its first answer, and only the wait of a refusal held. The times are counted on a clock of the application's own, the clock of the system read at the launch then moved on by the clock that never goes back (`src/trade/limits/clock.rs`): on Windows the second one alone can't tell a time before the start of the system (`library/std/src/sys/time/windows.rs` of Rust 1.95, read on 2026-11-22), so a request sent before a restart couldn't be counted. That clock stops while the system sleeps where the second one does (Linux and macOS, by the clocks the documentation of Rust names, `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` and `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW`; not measured): the file tells how far it was behind the clock of the system when written, and its times are read that much later, a request sent before the sleep then looking more recent than it is, never older. More recent is the safe side in a window, and the wrong one in the counts of the trade site, from which the requests it counted for sure are taken to find the hits of the other tools: the clock being the one of the whole file, once it shows that the times are read later than they were (one of them after the launch, or a file written by a clock more than a second behind), every request of the file stays in the windows and none is taken from those counts any more, so that the first answers after such a launch may count them twice and make a wait longer, never shorter (code reviews of 22p4). The log of the launch tells both cases. On Windows the clock that never goes back isn't set on the time of the network as the clock of the system is (recalled by the code review, not measured): a very long session may so fall a second behind without a sleep, with the same reading. Accepted: a clock set back by less than the time between the last time of the file and the launch can't be seen, its requests are read that much more recent and still taken from the counts, which hides the hits of another tool only if one fills a window right then. A file in doubt is read on the safe side: a time of the past that would come after the launch (a clock set back since, a file written by hand) is read as the launch, a window lasts a day at most, the requests are put in the order of their answers, and those no window counts any more are forgotten; with 30 searches in the file, a clock set back can so make the next search wait 5 minutes for nothing, once: a file with a time read as the launch is written again as it was read, right after its reading, so that the launch after doesn't start that wait over, and the log tells how many times were read so. Any other file is left as it is until a search leaves: what a launch read with a clock wrong that one time (ahead, its requests forgotten) isn't written. Only the end of a restriction more than a day ahead, which no answer can ask, is ignored, as the wait of a refusal is: it would stop the searches for as long as the clock was set back. A clock moved forward since makes the requests look older and frees the windows sooner, as it ends the wait of a refusal early: the counts the trade site tells in the first answer then bring its hits back. Left out: a request still without its answer when the application is closed isn't counted, though the 10 seconds of its search are, written when it leaves; two Exile Market that run at once don't share this file (the last one to write wins; each sees the requests of the other in the counts the trade site tells at each answer, as those of a browser); and the search block only shows a wait kept at the first click, as for the wait of a refusal.
- Never send a `POESESSID` session cookie: the searches work without an account. The trade site sets one in each answer; the HTTP client keeps no cookie (the `cookies` feature of `ureq` is off, which a test and a check of the CI, `.github/scripts/no-session-cookie.sh`, keep so). So no direct whisper and no travel to a hideout; **Copy whisper** only copies the whisper text of the listing to the clipboard.
- Send a `User-Agent` with the application name, its version and the repository address.
- The README states which services the application calls, and this risk.
